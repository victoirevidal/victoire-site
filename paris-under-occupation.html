Paris under Occupation

Everyday Lives in an Occupied City

About 3 hours Private walk Up to 6 guests English & French

Explore Paris during the German Occupation of 1940–1944 on a private history walk through the Left Bank with Victoire Vidal. Through streets and places connected to the Second World War, we consider how occupation changed everyday life — and the choices people faced. The route and emphasis are tailored to your interests.

The idea

The history of a city, through the lives of its people.

History can leave its mark on the most ordinary places: an apartment building, a school, a shopfront or a familiar street.

During the Occupation, Parisians continued to work, raise families and navigate daily life under restrictions, shortages and fear. But their experiences differed profoundly. Persecution, deportation, resistance and collaboration shaped this period, alongside the compromises and uncertainties of everyday survival.

This walk connects the wider history with individual lives and the places where events unfolded. From the German Occupation to the Liberation, we explore how the city changed, what its streets preserve, and how Paris remembers this chapter today.

The story behind this walk

How this walk came to be — in Victoire’s words.

Why create a walk about the Occupation?

Because the history becomes more tangible when we connect it with individual lives. Alongside the major events, I wanted to explore what this period meant for people in their homes, workplaces and neighbourhoods.

Why tell it through the streets of Paris?

Many buildings and streets from this period remain part of everyday Paris. They offer a starting point for understanding the past, alongside memorials and the stories of those who lived here.

How do you shape the experience?

Each walk combines historical context with individual stories, following your interests and allowing time for questions. The subject calls for care, and the experience leaves room for reflection as well as conversation.
